# 2. Methods (excerpt, fictional paper)

## 2.2 Marine heatwave detection

Marine heatwaves (MHWs) were identified following a threshold-based definition applied to daily
sea surface temperature (SST) at each grid point. A seasonally varying climatology and threshold were
constructed from the 1983–2012 baseline period. For each calendar day, the climatological mean and the
90th percentile were computed from all daily values within an 11-day window centred on that day,
pooled across all years of the baseline. Both the climatology and the threshold were then smoothed
with a 31-day moving average to remove day-to-day noise.

An MHW was defined as a period during which SST exceeded the 90th-percentile threshold for at least
five consecutive days. Two events separated by a gap of two days or fewer were considered a single
event. For each event we recorded the onset and decline dates, duration, maximum intensity (the
largest exceedance of the climatological mean), mean intensity and cumulative intensity (the integral
of the exceedance over the event). Event frequency was expressed as the annual count of events and
total MHW days as the annual sum of event durations.

Trends in annual MHW metrics were estimated by ordinary least squares and their significance assessed
with a two-sided t-test at the 95% level, accounting for serial correlation using an effective sample
size correction.
