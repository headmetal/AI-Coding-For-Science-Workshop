# 11 · Repurpose the text

**Time:** 10–15 min · **Difficulty:** warm-up · **Shows:** the same content for four audiences, and where it over-claims

## The situation
`source_text.md` has two pieces of writing: a dense paragraph from a model's technical documentation,
and a bullet list of results from an analysis. Both are fictional but realistic.

## Your task
**Part A, the dense paragraph.** Paste it and ask for, in one reply:
1. A plain-language summary for a programme manager with no physics background (80 words max).
2. A two-sentence version for a tweet or Slack post.
3. A NumPy-style docstring for a function `melt_pond_fraction(...)` that would implement the scheme.
4. Three questions a reviewer would ask about this scheme.

**Part B, the bullet list.** Paste it and ask for a paragraph suitable for the results section of a
paper. Then read it with a red pen. Mark every word that claims more than the bullets support.

## How to check
In Part B, a typical draft will upgrade "consistent with" to "demonstrates", "in two of three regions"
to "across regions", and will invent a mechanism that the bullets never mention. If you find none of
these, either the AI was unusually careful or you were not. Ask it: "Which sentences go beyond what I
gave you?" and compare with your red pen.

## Try this
- Ask for the Part A summary at three reading levels: 10-year-old, undergraduate, specialist. Watch
  what gets dropped at each level and whether anything gets *added*.

## Reflect
Rewriting is where AI is most fluent and least checked. Where in your own writing would you let it
loose, and where would you not?
