## Part A: technical documentation paragraph

The melt pond parameterisation follows a level-ice formulation in which the pond fraction on each
thickness category is prognosed from the balance of surface meltwater production, lateral drainage
through macroscopic flaws and vertical percolation through the ice matrix, the latter being
suppressed once the ice interior temperature falls below the brine-drainage threshold such that
the permeability, computed from the brine volume fraction via a cubic law, drops below a prescribed
critical value. The pond depth-to-area relationship is assumed linear with a category-dependent
aspect ratio, and ponds are permitted to refreeze when the net surface energy flux is negative for
longer than the pond-lid persistence timescale, after which the lid albedo replaces the pond albedo
in the shortwave radiative transfer until the lid thickness exceeds the optical penetration depth,
at which point the category reverts to bare-ice optics. Pond water that exceeds the hydrostatic
freeboard constraint is removed instantaneously to the ocean, with no associated salt or heat flux,
an approximation that is known to bias the summer surface salinity in the marginal ice zone.

## Part B: results bullets

- Trend in September sea-ice extent, 1992–2023: -1.1 ± 0.9 % per decade (not significant at 95%).
- Trend is negative in the Weddell and Ross sectors, positive in the Indian Ocean sector.
- Correlation between annual extent anomaly and the SAM index: r = 0.31 (p = 0.09).
- Model ensemble mean trend over the same period: -2.4 % per decade; 4 of 10 members overlap the observed range.
- Observed extent fell below the 1992–2023 mean in 7 of the last 8 years.
- Mixed-layer depth anomalies co-vary with extent anomalies in the Weddell sector only (r = 0.52, p = 0.01).
